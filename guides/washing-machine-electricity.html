FIELD NOTE 08 / APPLIANCES

What affects a washing machine’s electricity use?

Look beyond motor wattage to water heating, cycle choice, and load size.

By VoltLedger Editorial · Prepared 11 November 2026 · Educational estimate

The electricity used in laundry depends on the entire cycle, not a fixed label wattage. Water-heating choices, spin settings, cycle length, and whether drying is included can change the energy associated with one load.

Measure per load when possible

A washing machine’s motor does not draw a constant amount throughout a wash. A measured kWh-per-cycle figure for a given program provides a better comparison than running a nominal wattage for the whole cycle. Water heating may dominate on some machines and configurations.

When comparing two cycles, change only one meaningful variable if possible: temperature, program, load size, or spin setting. Note cleaning performance as well as energy use so a lower figure does not come at the cost of washing twice.

Think in weekly routines

One household may do four small loads while another batches two appropriately sized loads. The total is affected by whether the washer was underfilled, the amount of water heated, and the cycle choices. Avoid overfilling beyond manufacturer instructions.

Estimate weekly kWh by multiplying measured or documented kWh-per-cycle by cycles/week, then annualize using a realistic number of weeks. For dryer use, keep drying energy separate so you can see which process drives the cost.

Consider the whole cost of laundry

Water, detergent, clothing care, and the dryer all influence total operating cost. Electricity alone should not push you toward a practice that damages fabrics or conflicts with safe sanitation needs.

If a label offers annual energy consumption, check the underlying number of cycles and standard test conditions. Your results may differ substantially if you regularly use a very different program.

APPLIED EXAMPLE / NOT A UNIVERSAL RESULT

Work through the numbers

If a selected wash program consumes 0.65 kWh per cycle and you run five cycles each week for 50 weeks, the annual estimate is 162.5 kWh. At $0.23/kWh that models $37.38 in energy charges for washing, not including a dryer or water.

A small field checklist

Use these actions before making a purchase, changing a schedule or explaining an unexpectedly high bill. Keep a record of the assumptions that matter.

  1. Record cycles per week and program selected.
  2. Use per-cycle consumption when available.
  3. Compare water heating and spin settings separately.
  4. Keep clothes care and cleaning effectiveness in view.
Common mistake

Nameplate motor watts alone do not predict laundry energy per completed load.
