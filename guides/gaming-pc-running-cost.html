FIELD NOTE 13 / APPLIANCES

Gaming PC running cost: don’t use the power-supply wattage

Measure the workload that matters, then build a realistic usage profile.

By VoltLedger Editorial · Prepared 11 November 2026 · Educational estimate

A computer with a 750 W power supply is not constantly consuming 750 W. That figure is the power supply’s rated capacity, while actual consumption depends on the processor, graphics workload, displays, peripherals, and idle behavior.

Measure the entire system

For whole-system energy cost, a properly rated wall monitor is often more useful than a software display that estimates one component. Include monitors and sound devices separately if they are powered through different outlets.

A gaming workload varies by title, frame rate, resolution, and limits. Compare the same game and settings when evaluating an energy-saving change. Avoid opening the power supply or using unsafe electrical measurement methods.

Keep gaming and idle modes apart

A person gaming for two hours but leaving a desktop awake for ten hours has two usage patterns, not twelve gaming hours. Record active hours, idle hours, and sleep time.

An illustration using 330 W for gaming, 70 W for light work, and 8 W during sleep can be calculated separately. However, these figures are not representative of every PC; replace them with tested numbers if a purchase decision depends on the result.

Compare settings against experience

Frame caps, display brightness, and manufacturer-supported energy settings can change energy use without requiring a new machine. But the best setting depends on performance needs, accessibility, and hardware behavior.

Run repeated tests under similar conditions. A tiny one-session difference may be ordinary variation, so do not present it as proof of an annual saving.

APPLIED EXAMPLE / NOT A UNIVERSAL RESULT

Work through the numbers

A PC that draws 350 W for 2 hours and 70 W for 3 hours uses (350×2 + 70×3)/1000 = 0.91 kWh before displays. At 20 days per month, that is 18.2 kWh. A 750 W power-supply label would overestimate this example.

A small field checklist

Use these actions before making a purchase, changing a schedule or explaining an unexpectedly high bill. Keep a record of the assumptions that matter.

  1. Ignore the power-supply capacity as a usage estimate.
  2. Measure active, idle, and sleep modes separately.
  3. Keep game/settings consistent between comparisons.
  4. Include monitors and peripherals in whole-setup totals.
Common mistake

Rated PSU capacity is not a wattmeter reading.
