FIELD NOTE 07 / APPLIANCES

How expensive is a portable electric heater to run?

Calculate resistive heating cost while keeping electrical safety first.

By VoltLedger Editorial · Prepared 11 November 2026 · Educational estimate

A portable resistive heater can draw substantial power while its element is active. Unlike some electronics, a labeled 1,500 W setting may be a fairly useful upper-rate input, but its thermostat still changes actual runtime.

Estimate active heating time

At 1,500 W, one hour of continuous heating uses 1.5 kWh. Four hours at that draw means 6 kWh. However, if the thermostat cycles, the heater may consume less during the same wall-clock interval. Do not confuse the plugged-in duration with measured active heating.

In a simple scenario, a 1,500 W heater averaging two active hours/day for 25 days consumes 75 kWh. At $0.24 per kWh, the variable energy estimate is $18. The number does not include room heat loss, system alternatives, or fixed electricity fees.

Reduce the need rather than improvising equipment

Reasonable draft control and temperature management may reduce the hours a heater must run. Keep all manufacturer-required clearances, avoid covered outlets or damaged cords, and do not use makeshift adapters or extension cords contrary to instructions.

If you are uncertain about circuit capacity, outlet condition, or repeated breaker trips, stop and seek qualified advice. A cost calculator should never be used to decide that an electrical installation is safe.

Compare heating approaches on equal comfort

A whole-home system and a single-room heater provide different coverage. The cheaper hourly rate for one device is not an apples-to-apples comparison if one heats a small desk corner and the other heats an entire dwelling.

Record the space, duration, comfort target, and weather before comparing options. It is better to state “this models the running cost” than “this is the most efficient way to heat your home.”

APPLIED EXAMPLE / NOT A UNIVERSAL RESULT

Work through the numbers

A 1,500 W heater used at full draw for 90 minutes uses 2.25 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, that is $0.45 in modeled energy charges. A thermostat that cycles changes the figure, so an outlet timer alone does not show actual energy consumption.

A small field checklist

Use these actions before making a purchase, changing a schedule or explaining an unexpectedly high bill. Keep a record of the assumptions that matter.

  1. Check the manufacturer’s electrical and clearance instructions.
  2. Use a realistic estimate of active heating time.
  3. Calculate a range rather than a single promised bill.
  4. Treat electrical faults or overheating as safety concerns.
Common mistake

No energy-saving tip should involve defeating safety devices, opening a heater, or overloading an outlet.
